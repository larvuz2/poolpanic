Marina, in 3D for the first time

She had only pictures until now. Her design was drawn with GPT Image 2.5 from her story picture and Coach Rivers' model as references (no mouth, like the coaches), made 3D with Meshy 7 and rigged on Coach Waters' skeleton, so she plays every one of his clips. She stays out of the game itself: Marina never appears on the deck. Her model is kept with the poster tools, not in the game's files.

Brand book
One look, everywhere

    
Pool Panic already has a look: the bubbly yellow logo, the game's deep-pool interface, the glossy toy-like characters. This makes it a rule, so the store page, capsules, clips, social posts and the trailer all feel like the same game.


  
The logo

  

    
On sky / water: yes

    
On deep pool: yes

    
On cream: yes

    
Stretched: never

  

  

    
Do
    
      
  • Use the file as it is (dist/assets/brand/logo.webp), with its navy outline and splash.
    • 
      
  • Give it room: clear space of at least the height of the "O" all round.
    • 
      
  • Put it over sky, water or the deep-pool navy, with a soft navy shadow when the art is busy.
    • 
      
  • At small sizes (the 462×174 capsule and below), let it fill the frame.
    • 
    

    
Don't
    
      
  • Redraw, retype, recolour, stretch or rotate it.
    • 
      
  • Set it over a face, or over busy detail without its shadow.
    • 
      
  • Add a subtitle, laurels or "Wishlist now" on a capsule (Steam's rules forbid it).
    • 
      
  • Upscale the 640-pixel file for the library logo: ask for a master at 2560 wide or vector (see the strategy's list).

Strategy
From Coming Soon to launch

    
The short version: put the page up now and start collecting wishlists, ship a demo in spring, enter Steam Next Fest in June 2027 with a following already built (a game gets one Next Fest, ever), and launch after it. The dates are proposals; the store itself waits on what only the creator can do.


  

    
10,000
wishlists at launch: the floor

    
25,000+
the goal; first-month sales run about 0.22 per wishlist now

    
$14.99
suggested price, 15% off at launch (cap 40%)

    
EN + 简体
store page languages first (Simplified Chinese is a third of Steam)

  


  


  
Only the creator can do: the Steamworks account and the $100 app fee, the app id, the price, the release date, written proof that every AI tool used allows commercial use (Meshy, Tripo, fal's models, ElevenLabs), and the AI disclosure on the content survey. Nothing here can go live before that.


  

    
Demo
One week of the run with co-op on: the Community Pool and two venues, the first five incidents, Marina's first wish. Live about three weeks before Next Fest (the top 25 in October 2026 had demos up 19 days before). Never shadow-drop it.

    
Creators
Fit over volume: streamers who play Overcooked, PlateUp! and Two Point. Keys through Keymailer. Every incident's fail is a ready 5 to 10 second clip, and the shift's highlight replay can be cut vertically.

    
Festivals
Third-party festivals favour management and cosy games; apply to every one that fits. Skip Couch Co-Op Fest (it needs local co-op) and Shop Keeper Fest (a shop game's festival).

    
AI disclosure
Steam shows it on the page. Disclose plainly, and keep the capsule, clips and screenshots free of the generic AI look: our own models in our own engine, as here. In October 2026, 8% of Next Fest's top 25 disclosed AI against 35% of the rest.

  






  
Research
What the best pages do

    
Read from Steam's documentation and from the pages of fifteen comparable games (Overcooked! 2, Two Point Hospital, PlateUp!, Moving Out, Untitled Goose Game, Lethal Company, Content Warning, PEAK, R.E.P.O., Schedule I, Supermarket Simulator and others) on 4 November 2026.

  

    
Capsules
  • The logo must read at 120×45: big, two short words, thick outline. Pool Panic's does.
  • Comedy management sells on faces with big expressions (Two Point) and one clear joke (Goose Game).
  • Text other than the title on a capsule breaks the rules and loses featuring.

    
Short description
  • Say who you are and the funny verb ("…you are a horrible goose").
  • Put the player count in it (R.E.P.O. "up to 6 players").
  • Median 232 characters; ours are 254 to 259.

    
About section
  • The least-read part of the page: short, and mostly looping clips.
  • Breakout pages run 120 to 360 words, one clip per idea, jokey headers.
  • Wide, low strips as section breaks (PEAK, PlateUp!); make them 1170 wide.

    
Launch
  • Popular Upcoming now needs about 100,000 wishlists: build the base elsewhere.
  • Next Fest is crowded (7,240 games in October 2026): arrive with followers.
  • Friendslop hits price at $8 to $10; content-rich comedy management at $20 to $30.
